Goods decision-threshold diagnostic
==================================

Outcome
Increasing the goods decision threshold improves pixel-weighted precision and
goods IoU on the reused development cohort, while reducing recall and frame-mean
IoU. This is an inference operating-point change, not a training improvement.
The retained model and historical equipment-experiment rejection are unchanged.

Protocol
The six thresholds were declared before inference. Each arm uses its existing
three final checkpoints; training, references, normalization and image geometry
are unchanged. Native float32 logits are bilinearly resized with
align_corners=False before thresholding. A pixel is goods when the two-class
logit margin exceeds log(t/(1-t)); at 0.50 this preserves argmax ties as
background. Threshold probabilities are not calibrated confidence estimates.
Ignored targets remain excluded. One threshold is shared across all seeds;
no per-frame or per-seed threshold selection is permitted.

The declared exploratory selection maximizes mean seed IoU subject to precision
not falling below the same arm's 0.50 baseline, breaking ties by lower threshold.
It selects real-only 0.90 and synthetic 0.95. This rule deliberately does not
guarantee recall preservation and is separate from the historical utility rule.
No candidate is automatically promoted or accepted by this selection.

Tradeoffs
Synthetic 0.70 gains 1.57 pp IoU and 3.16 pp precision against its own 0.50
baseline, but loses 3.35 pp recall. Every seed gains aggregate IoU and precision.
Five frames gain mean IoU, five regress, and two remain unchanged. Frame 000753
loses 3.57 pp and 000376 loses 3.61 pp; the dark-goods benefit is partly lost.
Its frame-mean IoU falls from 41.64% to 41.20%.

Against the original real-only 0.50 control, synthetic 0.70 gains 1.36 pp IoU
and 1.70 pp precision, but loses 0.37 pp recall. When both arms use 0.70,
synthetic gains 0.56 pp IoU and loses 0.90 pp precision. Neither comparison
passes the historical no-precision-or-recall-regression rule.

Synthetic 0.60 exceeds the original real-only 0.50 aggregate metrics, but its
precision difference is only 0.0165 pp and its precision is below the real-only
0.60 arm. This development-selected difference does not reverse the original
rejection or establish synthetic utility. Independently selecting thresholds
for each arm is also not an isolated comparison of synthetic training benefit.

Synthetic 0.95 has the highest tested mean IoU, but recall drops to 73.36% and
seven frames regress in mean IoU. It is the grid boundary, not an established
optimum or a general segmentation solution. No 90% precision/IoU claim is met.

Verification and limits
All six checkpoints strictly reload. All 72 original native prediction masks
are reproduced pixel for pixel and all 432 threshold-mask exports are recounted
after lossless PNG export. All 128 reference artifacts are reverified. Local
fixtures check background tie behavior, ignored pixels, known confusion counts
and rejection of nonfinite margins and invalid thresholds.

The existing pinned Python 3.12.15 / PyTorch 2.9.1+cu128 shared runtime was used
on the RTX 5070 Laptop GPU, one float32 frame per inference batch, four CPU
threads. Verification, inference and exports took 26.42 seconds. PyTorch peak
allocated GPU memory was 89,206,272 bytes; this is not total device memory use
or an accepted-sample throughput measurement. No packages were installed.

The same 12 dependent development frames support threshold selection. Repeated
development use and model-assisted reference provenance limit conclusions.
These are exploratory, development-selected results, not independent evidence
of generalization. No training, reference changes, locked-test evaluation or
model promotion occurred. Broader reviewed real coverage remains the priority
before another training experiment; threshold, data and loss changes must be
evaluated as separate interventions.
