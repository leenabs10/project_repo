Warehouse representation assessment
===================================

Qualification status
The training-only assessment is complete. Three paired geometry scouts are
feasible under the existing scene contract. No new RGB/depth/label batch has
been rendered, accepted or admitted, and no new model has been trained.
The preceding two-candidate utility campaign remains closed after its final
candidate failed the frozen requirements. This assessment does not supersede
that rejection or establish synthetic usefulness.

Observed representation gaps
The registered library has eight normalized mesh entries derived from four
original source objects: a carton, tyre, lattice crate and cement bag. Front/back
rotations are derivatives of the same source. The final candidate used only the
carton and tyre families as goods. The crate is handling equipment; the cement
bag remains exploratory, without a verified customer-inventory match.

Training examples show varied package proportions, close partial crops, adjacent
mixed stock, wrapping, image blur and distinct load/equipment boundaries. Current
opaque geometry covers only part of this appearance and content range. Native
training goods-mask fractions range from 0% to 31.48%, with median 10.22%.
These are visible class areas, not object sizes, distances, counts or sufficient
photographic coverage requirements.

The renderer already supports camera changes, source UV materials and mixing
registered opaque meshes. Earlier experiments also included close viewpoints
and mixed packaging. Repeating those ideas with the same source objects is not
a demonstrated new solution. The latest arrangement revision improved on its
predecessor but still reduced recall against the real-only control.

Verified and proposed capabilities
- Current verified interface: static opaque metric cuboids and registered meshes,
  pinhole cameras, RGB, axial depth, validity, semantic and instance outputs.
  Appearance qualification remains specific to each reviewed batch.
- Geometry feasibility only: closer carton and tyre views, plus a mixed
  carton/tyre bay, with corresponding equipment-only negatives. The proposed
  camera poses are outside older preset bounds; they require an explicit
  versioned resolver and fresh render/camera/depth/label fixtures.
- Asset work required: materially different source package designs, appropriate
  source textures and conversion/scale/UV qualification. Recolouring or rotating
  one carton does not establish independent source diversity.
- Support work required for irregular stacks: the current refiner verifies
  aligned equal-footprint contacts. Object rotation has no scene-field support;
  baked, registered orientation derivatives need their own geometry checks.
- Unsupported: transparent or loose wrapping. The material contract is opaque
  and the restricted glTF converter rejects non-OPAQUE material declarations.
  An opaque placeholder cannot qualify film, visible surfaces or hidden goods.
- Not qualified: motion blur and a matched photographic camera response. Current
  sensor-condition code implements RGB gain and depth-mask transfer; brightness
  variation does not qualify blur or sensor realism.
- Collection work required for broader experiments: a scene job permits at most
  eight captures, and current scene training consumes one admitted dataset/job.
  A larger collection needs explicit source-bound multi-job admission/input
  handling. Silent manifest concatenation is not an implemented capability.

Geometry scout result
Six unrendered resolved captures were prepared: three loaded cases and three
equipment-only counterparts. All satisfy the scene/task contracts and camera
containment check. Each received 880 coarse independent nearest-hit probes.
Close cartons, close tyres and mixed stock exposed 17, 9 and 18 goods instances
respectively on that grid. Empty counterparts exposed no goods. The mixed
feasibility scene explicitly adds six registered tyre instances; it is not a
continuation of the earlier fixed-inventory intervention.

These probes establish geometric feasibility only. They do not compare renderer
labels or depths, establish appearance, certify physical stability, or qualify
the proposed camera poses. The original cameras, samples and decisions remain
preserved in their own experiment snapshots.

Capability milestones for the next approach
1. Qualify a representative opaque asset library. Initial design needs are
   tall cartons, flat/wide cartons and worn or irregular opaque packages, grounded
   in training observations. A shipping-case family is conditional on explicit
   load/handling-container semantics; hidden contents are not inferred. These
   categories are proposed coverage requirements, not acquired or accepted assets.
2. Qualify six small render scouts using the new assets: close cartons, close
   tyres and mixed occupied bays, each paired with equipment-only context.
   Require native correspondence, independent geometry, contacts, visible labels
   and exact-batch human appearance/label decisions. Resolve RGB reproducibility
   or declare the control policy before execution; prior byte failures stay failed.
3. Resolve wrapping separately through a versioned feasibility specification,
   material/label/depth semantics and fixtures. Keep it unsupported until qualified.
4. Before testing a diversity hypothesis, prepare source-bound admitted-collection
   support. Thirty distinct loaded donors is a proposed design target matching
   the existing thirty mixed auxiliary presentations, not an adequacy guarantee.
   Preserve IDs, approvals, source bindings, split isolation and bounded job sizes.
5. Freeze a separate new campaign after asset, appearance and admission readiness.
   A broader representation intervention changes multiple factors and must be
   reported as such. Match initialization, primary real exposure, update budgets,
   seeds and selection policy. Retain the established utility requirements and
   report every seed/frame gain and regression. The previous campaign stays closed.
6. Complete independent final-reference qualification, frozen held-out evaluation
   and reproducible delivery only after development utility passes. No locked-test
   access is authorized or performed by this assessment.

Backend decision
The evidence does not require a simulator migration or cloud execution. Keep the
implemented Blender worker for supported opaque geometry while assessing assets
and fidelity requirements. BlenderProc and Isaac adapters remain unimplemented;
changing backend would still require asset/material conversion and camera,
geometry, depth, label and appearance qualification.

Remaining limits
The observed gaps are hypotheses and coverage limitations, not an isolated
explanation of the training regression. Neither passing geometry nor matching
class-area statistics establishes photographic similarity or useful training
data. Navigation, physical stability, calibrated sensor accuracy and simulated
LiDAR remain separate requirements. No data-complete or utility-release claim
is supported by this assessment.
