Goods synthesis failure analysis
===============================

Scope
The completed preserved-exposure candidate remains rejected: mean IoU 49.66%
for matched real replay versus 48.23% with rendered scenes. The diagnostic audit
profiles 30 real training references, eight admitted scenes and all 72 saved native
development predictions. Training coverage and development error diagnosis are
stored separately. No model is fitted and no target, source image or held-out
test payload is changed or evaluated.

Measured findings
Dark goods account for 10.05% of primary real foreground pixels, 11.06% of replay
auxiliary foreground and 6.35% of scene auxiliary foreground. These are raw pixel
exposure fractions, not gradient contributions. The existing loss separately
normalizes goods and background, so a pixel fraction alone does not describe its
effect on optimization. Dark is fixed display luminance below 50 on an 8-bit
Pillow RGB-to-L conversion; it is not calibrated sensor radiance.

The reviewed training frame Aisle_CCW_Run_1/000227 already labels a visible tyre
load as goods. Within one explicit diagnostic rectangle on those unchanged labels,
88.29% of the goods pixels are dark. Executed rendered tyre pixels are 45.62% dark
and contribute 12.45% of scene auxiliary foreground. A selected real region and
rendered visible-instance statistics are different populations. The real
rectangle is measured at native resolution; rendered counts use the 640x360
training derivative. This contrast
indicates a representation gap but is not a causal or dataset-wide estimate.

The two pure tyre views receive 7 of 60 auxiliary presentations; empty-equipment
and partial-occlusion views receive 20. Source-instance accounting includes tyre
surfaces in other views and avoids equating a scene name with every visible object.
All eight donors are visited, but the hash schedule does not enforce equal quotas.

Real training contains two background-only references, 000482 and 000880. They
remain in the primary stream. The current auxiliary loss requires both classes
and excludes them from replay donors. None of the eight rendered views is fully
background: the empty-equipment view still includes goods on the shelves. A
background-only auxiliary mode needs its own explicit normalization contract.

Native development errors
Mean miss rate on scorable dark goods increases from 26.45% to 40.41% across
seeds 17, 29 and 43. On the tyre-load frame 000753, the whole-frame goods miss
rate increases from 38.69% to 55.00%. These rates exclude ignored targets and
are separate from object-family-specific accuracy.

The background-only frame 000470 improves: false-positive rate decreases from
25.28% to 22.28%, although it remains high. Do not describe all background errors
as regressions or infer equipment-specific rates from binary foreground targets.
Per-frame false-positive and false-negative rates are retained for all twelve
development frames. Missing-class denominators remain null. A small positive
region, such as the 247 scorable goods pixels in 000941, limits interpretation
of frame-specific recall. Reference versions are unchanged.

Interpretation
Uneven donor exposure, insufficient dark foreground representation, appearance
differences and negative-example limitations remain plausible contributors. This
audit does not isolate their causal effects. Adding a tyre mesh and preserving
class mass did not establish a task improvement. Existing annotations must not be
changed solely because a candidate scores lower. Repeated development use and
related video frames limit independent generalization evidence.

Donor-schedule follow-up
The smallest interpretable contrast is a deterministic per-scene quota schedule
on the same accepted eight-scene export. Preserve source images and masks, the
reference cohort, all 450 primary real presentations, 60 auxiliary inputs, 120
updates, auxiliary loss weight 0.2, foreground class mass, initialization, three
seeds and final checkpoint policy. Derive quotas from training evidence and source
metadata only. Retain the completed replay and original scene-schedule results,
report every outcome, and require higher mean IoU without precision or recall
regression. Do not change appearance, loss and sampling together in this contrast.
This ablation has now been frozen, executed and independently audited. Mean IoU
rises from 48.23% to 49.38% but remains below replay at 49.66%; recall is 87.77%
versus 88.80%. It fails the utility rule. The additional tyre exposure does not
resolve dark-goods misses. See docs/sampling_benchmark_results.txt for all outcomes.
Scene expansion, broad reannotation and final held-out evaluation remain deferred.

Implementation and verification
CLI: python -m src.profiling.goods_failure_audit --help
The auditor requires explicit reference, control, scene and admission hashes. It
checks native prediction membership, source roles, RGB/target checksums, executed
input counts and all confusion totals before and after analysis. It never loads
a model. Training intensity uses BOX RGB and nearest-neighbour categorical masks
at 640x360; development partitions use native 1280x720 pixels.

The failure-audit verification passed 240 tests and 130 subtests; the completed
sampling extension passes 244 tests and 143 subtests. New fixtures cover half-open intensity bins,
ignored pixels, undefined rates, weighted exposure, invalid IDs/arrays, visible
source roles source mutation and mandatory snapshot identities. Original experiment and reference hashes remain
unchanged. Reports retain the full executed command bindings and fixture log.

Material investigation
Four isolated tyre-material captures preserve native labels and depth exactly.
The appearance response is modest; no training benefit or qualified realism is
claimed. See docs/tyre_appearance_qualification.txt for diagnostics and source bindings.

Equipment contrast execution readiness
The exact 30-training/12-development reference cohort is fully reverified. Six
empty/loaded renders and matched negative/mixed schedules pass input checks.
Pending decisions are rejected before admission; model performance remains
unmeasured for this intervention. See docs/equipment_pair_protocol.txt.

Completed equipment contrast
The six accepted paired captures reduce mean dark-goods misses (37.34% to 18.54%)
and improve the background-only frame's FP rate (22.52% to 19.80%). Aggregate
precision nevertheless falls from 55.86% to 54.40%; mean IoU falls from 51.63% to
51.42%. Recall rises from 87.42% to 90.40%. The candidate fails the utility rule.
These are matched three-seed results; binary masks do not identify every error's
object family. Existing references remain unchanged. Prioritize distinct real
training coverage before scaling this generator. See docs/equipment_pair_results.txt.
