Warehouse scene generation

Implemented capability
A portable resolved-scene contract and an isolated Blender Cycles worker generate
paired RGB, clean axial depth, validity, semantic and instance labels at 1280x720.
Geometry, materials, metric dimensions, optical camera poses, intrinsics and training
profile bindings are resolved before execution. Executables and storage roots are
deployment settings. Goods semantics live in a task adapter; they are not hardcoded
into shared scene contracts. Unsupported backends and modalities fail preflight.

Task-focused coverage
configs/warehouse_goods_coverage.json declares seven bounded cases: empty foreground
equipment, compact/wide/tall pallet loads, container occlusion, a lateral view of the
occluded load and a distant floor carton. Empty pallets and handling containers are
background; identifiable loads are goods. Stocked racks remain goods in every view.
These are related captures of one authored warehouse, not independent customer scenes.
The camera is explicitly pinhole with zero distortion; real export distortion and
registration ambiguity remain unresolved.

The planner validates finite dimensions, bounded controls, unique identifiers,
registered materials and explicit floor/pallet supports. It rejects floating support
requests, overlapping load bodies and goods penetrating equipment or structures.
The unchanged default planner still produces the previous three-capture geometry.
Carton sizes and positions are authored coverage intent, not customer measurements.
Only axis-aligned cuboid assets are supported; general mesh assets require a new
qualified adapter. Contact checks do not establish dynamics or load stability.

Verified geometry and visibility
The analytic 10 m plane establishes axial depth within 0.000001 m. Worker mesh rays
and another 1,792 independent analytic rays agree on instance identity. Maximum
per-view p95 analytic depth error is below 0.00005 m. Depth is float32 axial metres,
zero invalid, with a separate boolean validity mask. Native categorical PNG exports
round-trip exactly. Visible labels and clean depth derive from the same scene.

Independent carton silhouettes verify the requested targets. Compact, wide and tall
loads have approximately 4,281, 13,741 and 18,564 visible surface pixels. The two
container views have approximately 63.7% and 61.4% equipment occlusion; the distant
load retains 915 visible surface pixels. Silhouette checks omit bevels and allow
1 cm for attached tape/labels. These figures describe synthetic scenario coverage,
not sensor accuracy or statistical customer-domain coverage.

The full suite passes 201 tests and 80 subtests, including support, overlap, finite
control, label and known-occlusion fixtures. All 128 frozen reference artifacts are
unchanged. Existing annotations and retained models remain valid for their recorded
reference revision; this work does not require reannotation.

Surface assets and display response
Four original 4K Poly Haven color/roughness maps add wood and indoor floor detail.
Publisher size/MD5 and local SHA256 bindings match; catalogs retain source/license
URLs, color spaces and a 2 m tile scale. Floor color uses an explicit 0.25 palette
mix; wood uses the full map. These are authored controls, not measured photometry.
No normal or displacement maps are inferred. Shader bump affects RGB shading only.

The host verifies assets before allocation and after rendering; the worker verifies
contained paths and hashes before loading. Canonical exports embed the exact resolved
job and catalog. External texture bytes remain in the configured asset store. Job
document hashes isolate dataset versions while retaining scene-family grouping.
Changed job bindings, corrupt maps and invalid categorical payloads fail validation.

AgX remains primary. Standard is an unselected diagnostic saved from the same Render
Result; scene-linear EXR is retained. A known-radiance fixture explains why authored
strength 25 maps near 247/255 with AgX and clips with Standard at the same exposure.
The bright-pixel cutoff and training marginal statistics are diagnostics, not fidelity
certificates or admission gates. Customer camera response has not been calibrated.

Qualification and remaining capability
Visual inspection confirms the intended load/equipment arrangements and visible-label
separation, while the scene still looks procedural and repetitive. Natural appearance
and customer-domain fidelity remain unqualified. All scene outputs remain outside
training. Scene admission, a bounded accepted dataset and a matched real-only versus
hybrid experiment remain open; no scene-model improvement or promotion is claimed.

Blender 4.5.3 renders through the explicit blender_cycles backend on OptiX. BlenderProc
2.8.0 and Blender 4.2.1 are downloaded separately, but their renderer dependencies
could not initialize after package-index connection failures. BlenderProc remains
unavailable. Simulated LiDAR, moving scans, calibrated noise, physics and remote
execution are unsupported. Isaac remains a candidate backend requiring explicit asset,
material and sensor conversion fixtures, not an automatic or mandatory migration.

Local use
Configure runtime paths from configs/runtime/warehouse_scene.example.json and use
fresh output paths. Optional photographic surfaces require a configured asset_root;
workers do not download assets. Omitting --coverage-config retains the default pilot.

python -m src.synthesis.warehouse_scene --profile reports/dataset_profile/summary.json --camera-config configs/warehouse_renderer.json --asset-catalog .tools/scene_assets/surface_library/catalog.json --coverage-config configs/warehouse_goods_coverage.json --output reports/warehouse_coverage_job.json
python -m src.pipeline.scene --job reports/warehouse_coverage_job.json --runtime .tools/scene_surface_runtime.json --output reports/warehouse_coverage_run
